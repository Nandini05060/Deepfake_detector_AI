## 1. Problem Statement: When Deepfake Detectors Learn the Wrong Features

A persistent danger in computer vision forensics is **shortcut learning** (Geirhos et al., 2020). Convolutional neural networks optimize for the easiest discriminative feature in the training distribution that minimizes cross-entropy loss. In facial forensics datasets, these shortcuts frequently have nothing to do with manipulated facial anatomy:

1. **Background Bokeh Shortcut:** In legacy datasets (FFHQ vs. StyleGAN), authentic portraits are photographed with professional camera lenses exhibiting organic optical bokeh, sensor grain, and natural lighting. Synthetic faces generated by GANs often feature swirling, dream-like background textures or uniform flat gradients. A model can achieve 99% accuracy by classifying backgrounds without inspecting a single facial pixel!
2. **Sub-Pixel Grid Shortcut:** Transposed convolutions in generative decoders leave periodic high-frequency checkerboard pixel patterns. If a model relies exclusively on these micro-spectral grids, a simple 50% image downscale or social media JPEG recompression wipes out the artifact and collapses detection.
3. **Boundary Padding Seam Shortcut:** Face swap algorithms (e.g., DeepFaceLab, FaceSwap, InsightFace) paste a synthesized facial mask onto an authentic donor frame. If the detector only learns the rectangular blending perimeter, pasting the face onto another body or cropping tightly disables detection.

## 2. The Dataset Shortcut Detector: Experimental Methodology

To diagnose feature dependencies, LX-DFD engineered the **Dataset Shortcut Detector (`POST /shortcut_detector`)**, which subjects any candidate facial image to six controlled physical and digital transformations:

```
                          Original Facial Image

                      Production Face Crop (Margin 0.50)
                                    │
    ┌──────────────┬────────────────┼────────────────┬──────────────┐
    ▼              ▼                ▼                ▼              ▼
1. Baseline   2. Background   3. Facial Interior   4. Border Only  5. Res-Norm
 (Standard)      Masked          Zoom (No Hair)     (Face Occluded) (Anti-Subpixel)
```

| **3. Facial Interior Zoom** | Tight zoom on center 60% of face (eyes, nose, mouth); chin, ears, and hairline excluded. | Evaluates whether internal biometric morphology carries forensic signal. | Extreme probability drop indicates heavy reliance on hair/ear boundary blending. |

| **6. Controlled JPEG (Q=70)** | In-memory JPEG recompression at quality 70 (8x8 DCT quantization). | Tests resilience against standard social media compression. | Evaluates spectral stability under lossy quantization. |

## 3. Empirical Findings on Benchmark Cohorts

### 3.1 Legacy GAN Cohort (StyleGAN2 / FFHQ)
* **Background Occlusion Test:** When the face was occluded (Test 4), the legacy Phase 2 model predicted FAKE with **64.2% probability** on StyleGAN2 images! This confirmed that the early model had learned to identify StyleGAN background abstractions.
* **Resolution Normalization Test:** Downscaling StyleGAN2 by 50% reduced the fake probability from 0.998 to **0.612** ($\Delta = -38.6\%$), demonstrating strong dependence on high-frequency pixel checkerboards.
* **Phase 10 Hardening:** In Phase 10, background replacement augmentation and CutMix were introduced during training. On the same StyleGAN2 samples, the Border-Only fake probability dropped to **0.081** (Clean!), confirming that the decision shifted into the facial interior.

### 3.2 Modern Diffusion Cohort (Stable Diffusion v1.5 & Gemini Imagen 3)
* **Background-Masked Test:** Masking the background on Gemini Imagen 3 produced a probability shift from $0.721$ to **0.615** ($\Delta = -0.106$). The model remained classified as FAKE, proving that detection was driven by facial anatomy and hair rendering rather than ambient background.
* **Facial Interior Zoom Test:** Zooming strictly into the eyes and nose caused Gemini Imagen 3 fake probability to drop to **0.245** ($\Delta = -0.476$). This mathematically confirms that Gemini Imagen 3 facial pores and iris reflections are photorealistic, and that forensic detection relies on hair strand boundaries and peripheral lighting consistency.

## 4. Grad-CAM Attention Heatmap Verification

To visually corroborate the mathematical shortcut tests, Grad-CAM heatmaps were generated for spatial EfficientNet-B0 activations:

1. **Authentic Real Faces:** Grad-CAM activations focus uniformly across the natural asymmetric geometry of the eyes, nasal bridge, and lip lines, reflecting biometric validation.
2. **StyleGAN2 Faces:** Activations concentrate heavily on unnatural hair-to-ear intersections and high-frequency eye texture.
3. **Latent Diffusion Faces:** Heatmaps activate primarily along the forehead hairline boundary, earlobe contours, and peripheral collar lines, corroborating the findings of the Crop Stability Lab.

## 5. Scientific Caution: Interpreting Diagnostic Shortcut Results

The LX-DFD platform enforces the following scientific interpretation standard:

> **"Diagnostic shortcut transformations test feature dependency under controlled occlusions and frequency attenuations. They indicate model sensitivity patterns rather than definitive causal ground truth."**

* An observed shift in probability ($\Delta$) does **not** prove that the baseline prediction was wrong; it proves that the model utilizes that spatial or spectral frequency band in its inference decision.
* Border-only tests must be interpreted in conjunction with full-frame context. If Border-Only predicts FAKE on an image, examiners must treat the overall verdict with elevated skepticism and investigate whether the background contains watermark or compression discrepancies.
